MEMORIES OF MY SCHOOL YEARS / 01

Before the Bell

Tiancheng Ma · Inner Mongolia, remembered

I think it was 2016. I was fourteen. Some of the boys in my middle-school class came from settlements outside the county town, in Bayannur, Inner Mongolia. People called those places the First Regiment, the Second Regiment. I knew the names before I knew what a regiment was. I never did find out why the places were called that.

Their homes were too far away for them to go back each afternoon. They boarded at school and went home at weekends. Some had very little money. Others had trouble at home that reached the rest of us only in pieces.

Many seemed to have stopped trying in class. At the time, I thought this was something boys did at fourteen. Later I wondered whether they were tired in a way I had not yet learned to recognize.

One of them was Feng. His father was diagnosed with a terminal illness. It was already late.

A few days later, Feng left school.

I never saw him again.

Wang used to complain about the family he had been born into. His parents did not care about him, he said. He did not much want to go home.

Once he explained that there was no hot water there. He could not even have a proper wash.

I do not remember what I said to him. I remember the hot water.

He has since finished his bachelor’s degree and found a job. That is what I know of his life now.

I did well at school. This did not prevent Huang and me from arriving early in the morning to copy someone else’s homework.

There was homework every day. We had developed a reasonably dependable arrangement with the time before lessons. We were diligent about getting there early.

When I remember being a good student, I have to include this.

On the first day of middle school, our homeroom teacher said I was handsome. I have forgotten a great deal of what I was taught that year. That remark has survived without revision.

In the years that followed, there always seemed to be someone who liked me. I took this rather more for granted than I would care to admit now.

There was also Ma. We had the same surname, and our grandfathers were brothers. At school this seemed sufficient reason for us to be close. We had known something about each other before there was much to know.

In high school he became absorbed in video games. Eventually we stopped keeping in touch. I could not say when eventually began.

I am out of touch with almost everyone from those years. Middle school, high school: nearly all of them. There are people whose names I still know immediately, though I would not know what to ask them after hello.

The first semester of high school was a happy one. I liked my new classmates. We got along easily, and my marks were good. For a while, neither of these things seemed to require much explanation.

Our mathematics teacher had a sheet with everyone’s information and photograph on it. One day, looking over the photographs, the teacher said, “Let’s have the most handsome one come up and solve this. Tiancheng!”

I had now received independent confirmation from two schools. There was still the problem on the board.

Between lessons I read the winning essays from the New Concept Writing Competition. A break was long enough for a few pages. Then there would be another lesson, and afterward I could go back to where I had stopped.

Those are the days I remember as unhurried. We were in a Chinese high school; they cannot have been unhurried. But there was room in them for reading that would not be on the examination.

In the second semester, we took an exam that would sort us into advanced and regular classes. I was placed in an advanced class.

It was good news. I had to leave the people with whom I had been happy.

The new classroom had new teachers and new classmates. For a while I felt less capable there than my result suggested I ought to be. I had been moved because I was doing well. Now I had to learn how to be at school again.

After a time, I found my place. I got to know people. The work became manageable. A teacher chose me to represent the school on a television programme.

The first part of high school went well enough. There was pressure, certainly, but I could handle it. I had evidence that I could.

Then I was injured playing ball.

For several months I could not walk normally. I did not have surgery at the time, and the injury had still not properly healed when I finished high school.

School continued. There was homework every day.

My marks fell. The rest of my life became difficult in ways that did not fit as neatly on a results sheet. I had been accustomed to managing. I kept trying to do what I had done before.

There was no particular day on which I stopped expecting to be back to normal soon. The examinations simply came closer.

I stayed through the end. I sat the gaokao, the university entrance examination. I got into university.

Wang is working now. I am studying in Germany. Ma and I still have the same surname.

There is usually something to do. A message can wait until the weekend.

Of Feng, I have no later news.

When I think of those years, I sometimes find myself back at school before the first lesson, with Huang, copying the homework.

We still had a little time.
